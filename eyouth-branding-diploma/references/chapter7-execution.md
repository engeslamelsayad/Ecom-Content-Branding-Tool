## 7.1 Client Discovery Blueprint

The 4 core questions that anchor every brand strategy engagement:

1. What is the core business entity, and what structural market gap does it fill?
2. What are the top 3 aspirational brands you look up to, and specifically why?
3. What 3 industry players are direct competitors or strategic opposites?
4. What specific emotions do you want to create on a customer's FIRST interaction?

---

## 7.2 The 4-Column User Profile

Build at least 2 user profiles per brand. Use this exact structure:

| Column 1: Demographics | Column 2: Backstory & Psychographics | Column 3: Friction Points & Needs | Column 4: Strategic Solutions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Name (fictional), Age, City, Role, Income | Lifestyle, values, daily habits, routines, aspirations | Real personal struggles connected to the product category | Exactly how your brand removes friction and exceeds expectations |

**Cairo Example:**
- Sarah, 34, Maadi Cairo, Head of HR, High Income
- Career-driven, balances family, values minimalist quality, researches purchases carefully
- No time to shop, hates unreliable delivery windows, distrusts low-quality options
- Premium app with 3D virtual room planning + guaranteed 2-hour delivery windows

## 7.3 Market Lifecycle Position

| Phase | Characteristics | Brand Strategy Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | New category, zero awareness | Heavy education + simple value proposition |
| **Growth** | Rapid expansion, new customers discovering category | Build brand recognition + capture market share fast |
| **Maturity** | Growth stabilizes, competition peaks | Sharp differentiation + protect loyalty |
| **Decline** | Demand drops | Reposition into adjacent category or new market |

## 7.4 Brand Architecture Models

| Model | Structure | When to Use | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Branded House** | One master brand across all products | Unified equity, strong parent brand | Apple (Apple TV+, Apple Watch, Apple Pay) |
| **House of Brands** | Independent brands, hidden parent | Distinct audiences, no spillover risk | Unilever (Dove, Axe, Lipton) |
| **Hybrid / Endorsed** | Unique brands + visible parent backing | Balance of independence and credibility | Google (YouTube, Waymo + Google Pay) |

**Selection Decision Tree:**
- Is the master brand strong enough to elevate sub-products? → Branded House
- Do the products target completely different audiences that shouldn't overlap? → House of Brands
- Do products need independence but would benefit from master brand credibility? → Hybrid

## 7.5 The Benefit Prioritization Framework

Map every product feature into this matrix to guide product and marketing priorities:

| Tier | Features | Functional Benefits | Emotional Benefits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Will Buy (Strategic Driver)** | Unique differentiating capabilities | Solves an explicit painful problem | Creates passion, builds lifelong fans |
| **Adds Value (Nice-to-Have)** | Polished features improving experience | Operational bonuses improving daily use | Style, convenience, extra delight |
| **Expected (Baseline Entry)** | Standard industry features | Basic functional utility | Simple security, baseline relief |

**Strategic Rule**: Marketing budget goes to "Will Buy" features. "Expected" features are never highlighted — they are proof of legitimacy, not differentiation.
