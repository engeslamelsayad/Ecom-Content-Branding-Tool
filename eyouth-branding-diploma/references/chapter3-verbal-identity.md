# Chapter 3 — Verbal Identity & Naming Strategy

## /verbal Command — Full Execution Protocol

Deliver in this order: Naming Analysis → Voice Chart → Archetype Selection → Brand Story

---

## 3.1 The 6-Point Name Validation Checklist

Run every name candidate through ALL six tests:

| Test | Criteria | Pass Condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Pronunciation** | Short, effortless, balanced syllables | Can be said clearly first time by a stranger |
| **Domain Availability** | Exact-match .com preferred | Available or acquirable; no major squatter |
| **Search Bar Test** | Distinctive enough to own search results | Doesn't compete with dictionary words or dominant brands |
| **Lexical Expansion** | Can it grow into a brand language? | Can spawn verbs, nouns, culture (e.g., Tweet, Googling) |
| **Cliché Avoidance** | Free of overused industry buzzwords | No "Pro", "Hub", "Smart", "Nexus", "Tech" unless intentional |
| **Global Adaptability** | No offensive meanings in other languages | Test Arabic, French, German, Mandarin at minimum |

---

## 3.2 The 7 Naming Typologies

For each, generate 3 candidate names for the brand in context:

| Typology | Definition | Real Examples | When to Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Founder** | Creator's name builds personal heritage | Walt Disney, Ralph Lauren, Azza Fahmy | High personal brand credibility; founder IS the differentiator |
| **Descriptive** | Tells exactly what it does | Money Fellows, Truecaller, General Motors | Clarity over creativity; functional market entry |
| **Fabricated** | Invented word, fully unique and trademarkable | Xerox, Jumia, Kleenex | Maximum legal protection; building a new category |
| **Metaphor** | Real word suggesting a quality or feeling | Dove (purity), Amazon (vast), Apple (accessible) | Emotional resonance; ownable mental imagery |
| **Acronym** | Long name compressed to initials | NASA, IBM, CNN, FIAT | Complex institutional names; professional B2B |
| **Creative Spelling** | Deliberate misspelling for uniqueness | Swvl, valU, Dribbble | Strong digital identity; easy to trademark |
| **Numeric** | Numbers embedded in name | 7Up, Seven Secrets, 99designs | Origin stories; specific formulas; memorable counts |

**Recommendation Protocol**: Generate 2-3 options per typology, then shortlist to top 3 overall based on the validation checklist. Present with rationale.

## 3.6 The Brand Story — 3 Strategic Lenses

Every great brand story must contain 4 pillars: **Credibility + Differentiation + Purpose + Personality**

Choose or combine these lenses based on brand stage:

### Lens 1: Chronological Retrospective (Story After the Event)
- For: Established brands with heritage
- Content: Founding story, breakthroughs, historic failures, defining moments
- Tone: Reflective, earned, honest about struggles
- Structure: Origin → Defining Challenge → Turning Point → Current Reality

### Lens 2: Contemporary Narrative (Story During the Event)
- For: Brands in active transformation or early growth
- Content: Current mission in real-time, live battles, active achievements
- Tone: Urgent, transparent, "we're in it together"
- Structure: Current Reality → The Problem We're Solving → Our Approach → Invitation to Join

### Lens 3: Fundamental Descriptive (Story of the Brand's Core Belief)
- For: All brands — this is the bedrock story
- Content: The single belief that drives existence, the philosophy, the "why"
- Tone: Timeless, philosophical, conviction-driven
- Structure: What we believe → Why the world needs this → How we live it → What we ask of you

**Arabic Story Formatting Note**: In Egyptian Arabic, favor warmth and direct address (أنت/إنت). In Saudi Arabic, maintain respectful formality. In MSA, keep neutral gravitas appropriate for press and formal materials.
