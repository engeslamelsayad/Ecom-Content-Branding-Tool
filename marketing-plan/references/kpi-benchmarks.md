### COD vs. Prepaid Split (Egypt)
- Industry average: 60–70% COD
- Return rate on COD: 25–40%
- Return rate on prepaid: 5–10%
- **Implication:** CAC economics favor prepaid incentivization; budget 15–20% of marketing spend toward COD-to-prepaid conversion offers

## Healthy Business Ratios

### LTV:CAC Benchmarks
| Ratio | Interpretation |
|-------|----------------|
| Below 1:1 | Losing money per customer |
| 1:1 – 2:1 | Marginal, risky |
| 3:1 | Minimum healthy threshold |
| 4:1 – 5:1 | Strong growth business |
| 6:1+ | Exceptional unit economics |

### Gross Margin Minimums by Strategy
- Paid acquisition-dependent brand: GM must be >50% (ad costs will compress margins)
- Organic/influencer-led brand: GM can be 35–45%
- Premium/luxury positioning: GM should be 65%+

### Payback Period Benchmarks
- Bootstrapped: Target <3 months CAC payback
- Funded: Target <6 months CAC payback
- Enterprise: Target <12 months CAC payback

## Seasonality Calendar (MENA E-Commerce)

### Egypt
- **Peak periods**: Ramadan (March–April), Back to School (Aug–Sep), Eid shopping (Mar/Jun), 11.11, Black Friday, Christmas/New Year
- **Low periods**: January–February post-Eid, mid-summer heat (July–August lower CPMs)

### Saudi Arabia
- **Peak periods**: Ramadan, Eid Al-Fitr, Eid Al-Adha, Saudi National Day (Sep 23), White Friday (Black Friday equivalent), 12.12
- **Note**: Ramadan CPMs rise 30–50% but conversion rates also rise for right categories

### UAE
- **Peak periods**: Dubai Shopping Festival (Jan–Feb), Ramadan, Eid, White Friday, 12.12, New Year's

### Strategic implication
Always build a seasonality overlay into the Media Plan. Ramadan is the single highest-opportunity period for most consumer categories in MENA — plan creative specifically for it 6–8 weeks in advance.
