# Copywriting Frameworks Reference — 25+ Frameworks

## Framework Selection Matrix

| Awareness Stage | Market Sophistication | Best Frameworks |
|-----------------|----------------------|----------------|
| Unaware → Problem Aware | 1–2 | AIDA, AIDCA, The Big Promise Open, Story Hook |
| Problem Aware | 1–3 | PAS, TOR, BAB, Emotional Benefit Ladder |
| Solution Aware | 3–4 | 4Ps, FAB-A, Unique Mechanism, QUEST |
| Product Aware | 4–5 | 4Us, Objection-First, Comparison, Star-Story-Solution |
| Most Aware | 5 | SLAP, Direct Offer, Urgency Stack, Reverse Offer |
| All Stages | Any | PASTOR, The Big Idea, The Control Framework |

---

## SECTION A: Core Direct Response Frameworks

### 1. AIDA
**Best for:** Cold traffic paid social — Unaware to Problem Aware.

- **A — Attention:** Shatter the scroll. Aggressive, punchy hook that breaks feed pattern.
- **I — Interest:** Pivot to reader's real-life friction. Show profound understanding of their world.
- **D — Desire:** Paint vivid after-state. Status, comfort, convenience — make it specific.
- **A — Action:** Single explicit CTA demanding immediate response.

---

### 2. AIDCA (AIDA + Conviction)
**Same as AIDA + insert before Action:**
- **C — Conviction:** Hard validation — clinical metrics, media logos, expert credentials, verified counts. Eliminates logical resistance before the ask.

**When to add C:** High-ticket items, health claims, skeptical audiences, anything requiring trust establishment.

---

### 3. PAS — Problem / Agitate / Solution
**Best for:** Landing pages, advertorials, long-form email. Most reliable long-form framework ever written.

- **P — Problem:** Name the acute, specific pain. Generic pain = weak copy.
- **A — Agitate:** Twist the knife. How does this pain compound over time? What does it cost them daily? What future does it lead to if unresolved?
- **S — Solution:** Introduce the product as the inevitable, permanent mechanism that erases this specific pain.

**Agitation Escalation Techniques:**
- "And the worst part is…"
- "Every single day you don't solve this, [cost X] keeps accumulating"
- "Imagine [specific negative future] 6 months from now if nothing changes"
- "You've tried [X], [Y], [Z] — none of it worked. Here's why."

---

### 4. PASTOR (Extended PAS)
**Best for:** Long-form landing pages, VSLs, email sequences. PAS evolved.

- **P — Problem:** Define the pain precisely.
- **A — Amplify:** Expand consequences. What's at stake if unresolved?
- **S — Story/Solution:** Real customer story demonstrating the transformation.
- **T — Transformation:** Paint the vivid after-state in detail.
- **O — Offer:** Present what they get. Full stack — product + bonuses + guarantee.
- **R — Response:** Tell them exactly what to do next. Remove all ambiguity.

---

### 5. TOR — Transformation / Offering / Response
**Best for:** Long-form video scripts, VSLs, advertorials. Narrative-heavy.

- **T — Transformation:** Lead with a "Hell to Heaven" customer journey story. Specific avatar, specific situation, specific result.
- **O — Offering:** Exact package, inclusions, mechanism explanation, financial terms.
- **R — Response:** Crystal-clear next steps. No ambiguity between reading and clicking.

---

### 6. BAB — Before / After / Bridge
**Best for:** Short-form ads, email openers, social posts, short hooks.

- **Before:** Prospect locked in current frustrating reality. Specific and relatable.
- **After:** Frictionless, idealized future — problem completely gone.
- **Bridge:** Your product = the only architectural path connecting those two realities.

**Visual pairing:** Works perfectly with split-screen or side-by-side creative.

---

### 7. FAB-A — Features / Advantages / Benefits / Action
**Best for:** Product pages, LP body copy, below-the-fold sections.

- **F — Feature:** What the product physically IS or DOES. (The "what")
- **A — Advantage:** Why that feature outperforms legacy alternatives. (The "so what")
- **B — Benefit:** Raw emotional or economic value to the user. (The "what's in it for me")
- **A — Action:** Direct prompt to the conversion funnel.

**The "So What" Test:** After every Feature, ask "So what?" until you reach the emotional benefit. That's your copy.

Example:
- Feature: "Dual-layer foam sole"
- Advantage: "Absorbs 3x more impact than standard EVA foam"
- Benefit: "Your knees and lower back stop hurting after mile 3"
- Action: "Choose your size →"

---

### 8. The 4 Ps Architecture
**Best for:** Solution Aware prospects who need vivid outcome framing + proof.

1. **Picture:** Specific, sensory, immediate description of the ideal end-state.
2. **Promise:** Explicit declaration of how your product delivers that reality.
3. **Prove:** Undeniable objective proof (study data, verified counts, media logos).
4. **Push:** Strategic urgency that compels immediate action.

---

### 9. The 4 Us Headline Framework
**Best for:** Headlines, subject lines, call-out sections, micro-copy.

Rate every headline 1–4 on each dimension:
- **Useful:** Does it offer immediate functional value?
- **Ultra-Specific:** Can you add a number, timeframe, or quantified claim?
- **Unique:** Does it say something no competitor can replicate?
- **Urgent:** Is there a hard, logical reason to act NOW?

Score under 2 on any dimension = rewrite.

---

### 10. SLAP — Scroll-Stopper / Look / Action / Purchase
**Best for:** Low-ticket, high-velocity e-com impulse buys. Mobile-first.

- **S — Scroll-Stopper:** Visual or audio hook that halts thumb movement in 0.5s.
- **L — Look:** 3-second value prop consumption. What is it? Why does it matter?
- **A — Action:** Frictionless single micro-interaction (tap, swipe, click).
- **P — Purchase:** Zero-surprise checkout with trust signals + guarantee visible.

---

## SECTION B: Advanced Structural Frameworks

### 11. QUEST — Qualify / Understand / Educate / Stimulate / Transition
**Best for:** Cold traffic landing pages, advertorials, complex solutions.

- **Q — Qualify:** Filter immediately. "This is ONLY for [specific avatar]."
- **U — Understand:** Demonstrate deep empathy. Show you've lived their struggle.
- **E — Educate:** Teach them something valuable that reframes the problem. (Reciprocity)
- **S — Stimulate:** Activate desire with vivid outcome visualization.
- **T — Transition:** Smooth, logical bridge to the offer. Make buying feel inevitable.

---

### 12. Star-Story-Solution (SSS)
**Best for:** Email openers, ad copy with narrative, testimonial framing.

- **Star:** Introduce a character (customer avatar, founder, or the prospect themselves) who has the exact same struggle.
- **Story:** Tell their journey. Build tension. Make failure feel real.
- **Solution:** Reveal how your product resolved everything. The product as the hero.

---

### 13. The Reverse Offer / Anti-Pitch
**Best for:** Highly sophisticated, skeptical, or ad-fatigued audiences.

Open by explicitly telling them NOT to buy. Then systematically build the case for why they should. Creates massive curiosity and authority.

Structure:
1. "Don't buy [product] if…" (list real disqualifiers)
2. "We built this ONLY for [hyper-specific avatar]"
3. "Here's exactly what you're getting — no hype, just the truth"
4. The offer, stated plainly

---

### 14. The Objection-First Framework
**Best for:** Retargeting audiences, high-ticket sales, email follow-up.

Open with the #1 objection directly. Acknowledge it fully. Then dismantle it with proof.

Template:
1. "I know what you're thinking. '[Exact objection verbatim]'"
2. "And here's the honest answer: [acknowledge any truth in the objection]"
3. "But here's what changes everything: [proof/data/mechanism that neutralizes it]"
4. Offer + CTA

---

### 15. The Unique Mechanism Framework
**Best for:** Saturated markets (Sophistication Level 3+). Differentiates from competitor claims.

Steps:
1. Acknowledge the existing market claims everyone makes
2. Explain WHY those claims fail or are incomplete
3. Introduce your Proprietary Mechanism — give it a specific name
4. Explain the mechanism in simple, vivid, credible terms
5. Show why only YOUR product delivers this mechanism
6. Transition to offer

**Naming your mechanism:**
- Use specific, descriptive language
- Can be technical (credibility) or experiential (relatability)
- Examples: "Tri-Phase Absorption System" / "Cold-Press Extraction Method" / "24-Hour Cortisol Reset Protocol"

---

### 16. The Emotional Benefit Ladder
**Best for:** Identifying the REAL selling message. Used in copy development, not output.

Climb the ladder from functional to emotional to identity:
```
Product Feature
    ↓
Rational Benefit (what it does)
    ↓
Emotional Benefit (how it makes them feel)
    ↓
Identity Benefit (who it makes them)
    ↓
THE REAL REASON THEY BUY
```

**Rule:** Copy that speaks to the Identity level outperforms copy at the Feature level by 3–10x.

Example (ergonomic chair):
- Feature: Lumbar support adjustment
- Rational: Reduces back pain during long work sessions
- Emotional: Feel comfortable and focused all day
- Identity: "I'm someone who takes my health and performance seriously"
- **Real message:** "The chair for people who refuse to let physical limitations hold back their ambitions"

---

### 17. The Open Loop Framework
**Best for:** Email subject lines, video hooks, long-form content, serialized campaigns.

Opens a question or tension in the reader's mind that demands resolution. The brain is wired to close open loops — it cannot rest until the answer is revealed.

**Techniques:**
- "The real reason [common belief] is actually destroying your [desired outcome]..."
- "What I discovered after [timeframe] of [struggle] changed everything. Here's what it was..."
- "Most people do [X]. The ones who succeed do the opposite. And here's why..."
- Start a story, pause it for "I'll explain in a second" — then build before resolving

---

### 18. The Comparison / Challenger Framework
**Best for:** Side-by-side creative, LP sections, email content.

Structure:
1. Establish the "Old Way" (status quo, legacy solution, DIY)
2. List its real limitations honestly
3. Introduce the "New Way" (your product)
4. Map point-by-point superiority
5. Frame switching as obviously logical, not a hard decision

---

### 19. The Fear-Forward Framework
**Best for:** Problem Unaware audiences, B2B urgency, deadline-driven campaigns.**

Paint the cost of inaction in vivid, specific, future-paced terms.

Structure:
1. Current moment (status quo feels fine)
2. Hidden consequence (what's slowly getting worse without them knowing)
3. Future projection (what life looks like in 3 / 6 / 12 months if nothing changes)
4. The turning point offer (act now before it's too late)

---

### 20. The "One Big Idea" Framework (Gary Halbert / Eugene Schwartz)
**Best for:** Campaign-level strategy, VSLs, launch sequences.

Every campaign needs ONE single, compelling, surprising idea at its core.
The idea must be: **True + Interesting + Believable + Unique**

Test: Can you explain the core idea in one sentence that would make a stranger say "really? Tell me more"?

If not — you don't have a Big Idea yet. Keep digging.

## SECTION C: Format-Specific Frameworks

### 21. The Advertorial Framework
**Best for:** Cold traffic bridge pages, pre-lander pages, native ads.

Structure:
1. Native-looking editorial headline (no obvious ad markers)
2. Relatable story hook (journalist discovery / personal experience)
3. Problem exposure with depth
4. Expert or authority introduction
5. Mechanism reveal
6. Social proof integration
7. "Soft" offer with editorial framing
8. CTA that feels like logical next step, not a pitch

---

### 22. The Lead Type System
**Best for:** Choosing how to OPEN any piece of long-form copy. Match to awareness level.**

| Lead Type | Best For | Opening Logic |
|-----------|----------|---------------|
| **Problem Lead** | Problem Aware | Open directly with the pain |
| **Promise Lead** | Solution/Product Aware | Open with the big outcome |
| **Story Lead** | All stages | Open with a specific human moment |
| **Secret Lead** | Unaware | "What [authority] doesn't want you to know..." |
| **Prediction Lead** | All stages | "By [date], [X] will change everything" |
| **Conviction Lead** | Most Aware | "I'm going to prove [bold claim] in the next 3 minutes" |
| **Question Lead** | Problem Aware | Open with the question they're already asking themselves |

---

### 23. The Bonus Stacking Framework
**Best for:** Offer pages, sales pages, email promotions.

Stack value above the perceived price:
1. Core product (establish main value)
2. Bonus 1: solves a related but distinct pain
3. Bonus 2: removes a key friction from using the core product
4. Bonus 3: enhances the core result
5. Reveal total stack value (dramatically higher than price)
6. "If you got just [Bonus 1] alone, it would be worth [price]"

---

### 24. The Specificity Formula
**Not a framework — a RULE that applies to all frameworks.**

**Vague claims = zero credibility. Specific claims = high credibility.**

| ❌ Vague | ✅ Specific |
|---------|-----------|
| "Thousands of customers" | "47,382 verified buyers" |
| "Works fast" | "Results in 72 hours or your money back" |
| "Highly effective" | "84% of users reported [specific outcome] within 30 days" |
| "You'll feel better" | "Back pain reduced by an average of 67% in clinical user trials" |
| "Big savings" | "Save $240 per year vs. buying [alternative]" |

**Apply specificity to:** counts, timeframes, percentages, dollar amounts, named customers, named places.

---

### 25. The Price Reframe Framework
**Best for:** High-ticket objection handling, LP pricing sections, ad copy.**

Never defend a price — reframe it against something larger.

**Reframe Techniques:**
- **Daily cost:** "$3.30 per day — less than your morning coffee"
- **Cost of inaction:** "The average person spends $X/year on [inadequate alternatives]"
- **Cost of the problem:** "One [consequence of pain] costs $X. This costs $Y."
- **Per-use:** "Spread over 12 months, each use costs only $X"
- **Comparison anchor:** "Competitor X charges $Y. We charge $Z — here's what you get extra."
