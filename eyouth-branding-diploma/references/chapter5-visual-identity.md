## 5.1 The Stylescape Methodology

A Stylescape is a curated panoramic visual canvas — NOT a mood board. It combines:
- Photography style references
- Texture and material samples
- Typography layout combinations
- Color palette explorations
- Interface/packaging mockups

**First Stylescape** (Exploration): 3 contrasting visual directions — test what resonates
**Final Stylescape** (Production): One unified, polished visual system — the design team's blueprint

When guiding a client, describe the stylescape in words:
- Describe the dominant feeling (e.g., "warm earthy textures meeting sharp geometric type")
- Name the photography style (e.g., "documentary candid" vs. "studio clean white")
- Reference comparable brand visual worlds (e.g., "the warmth of Aesop meets the clarity of Notion")

## 5.2 Logo Typology Recommendation

| Typology | Description | Examples | When to Recommend |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Logomark (Symbolic)** | Abstract shape/icon, no text | Nike Swoosh, Apple, McDonald's arches | Strong visual culture, global scale, icon-first recognition |
| **Logotype (Wordmark)** | Name as the logo, custom typography | Coca-Cola, FedEx, Google, NASA | Name IS the brand asset; clarity and recall are paramount |
| **Combination Mark** | Symbol + text locked together | Adidas, MasterCard, Burger King | Early-stage brands building recognition; most versatile |

**Recommendation Rule**: Startups and new brands → Combination Mark (builds name recognition while establishing icon). Established brand with strong name equity → Logotype. Global icon-stage brand → Logomark.

## 5.3 Typography Architecture

| Family | Character | Signal | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Serif** | Decorative end strokes | Heritage, authority, premium elegance | Finance, Law, Luxury, Academia |
| **Sans-Serif** | Clean, geometric, no strokes | Modern, digital, accessible, tech-forward | Startups, Apps, FMCG, D2C |
| **Display/Script** | Expressive, personality-driven | Creativity, humanity, artisan quality | F&B, Beauty, Fashion, Creative Agencies |

**Pairing Rule**: Never mix two serifs or two sans-serifs. Pair a serif headline with sans-serif body, or vice versa, for visual hierarchy without conflict.

## 5.4 Color Psychology Matrix

| Color | Psychological Signal | Ideal Sectors |
|---|---|---|
| **Navy Blue** | Corporate expertise, stability, calm trust | Finance, Enterprise Tech, Legal |
| **Emerald Green** | Natural harmony, growth, wellness, wealth | Organic, Sustainability, Medical |
| **Deep Crimson Red** | Energy, passion, urgency, stimulation | Fast Food, Automotive, High-Impact Media |
| **Vibrant Orange** | Warmth, creativity, energy, accessible health | E-Commerce, FinTech, Children's |
| **Rich Purple** | Premium luxury, mystery, spiritual depth | Cosmetics, Premium Chocolates, Creative |
| **Pure Black** | Authority, sophistication, absolute premium | Luxury Fashion, High-End Tech |
| **Clean White** | Simplicity, purity, clinical precision | Health, Minimalist D2C, Tech |

**Production Color Models:**
- **CMYK**: Physical print, packaging, business cards
- **RGB**: Digital screens, websites, apps, social
- **HSB**: UI design, creating color variations
- **Grayscale**: Testing contrast and visibility before finalizing

## 5.5 Brand Guidelines Structure

A brand guidelines document must contain, in order:
1. Brand Story (1 page — the "why" before the "what")
2. Logo Usage: Safe zones, minimum sizes, approved/prohibited variations
3. Color System: Primary, secondary, neutral palettes with HEX/CMYK/RGB codes
4. Typography: Font families, hierarchy, sizing scales, web alternatives
5. Photography Style: Subject, composition, color treatment, mood
6. Tone of Voice Summary (3 traits, DO/DON'T table)
7. Misuse Examples: Visual examples of what NOT to do
